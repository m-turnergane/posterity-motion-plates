## D. Family, king, and no trial
- D1, never tried and never heard: DOCUMENTED. S3: "sem ekkor, sem később nem hallgattak ki". S2 adds that she demanded to be heard ("holott ezt ő nyomatékosan követelte"). Unchanged from v2.
- D2, the king pressed and Thurzó prevented it: single strand, S1 only: "Matthias repeatedly insisted on the charge of murder even though Thurzo continued to prevent a trial". Attribute it as "by one account", or keep the v2 wording.
- D3, family agreement: her son and sons-in-law were at the arrest (S1); Nikola Zrinski, her son-in-law, took part (wp-nikola6). The motive is INTERPRETATION with two camps: to protect the family's honour and property (S1, guilt-leaning), or a frame-up and property grab (S4; Hetyéssy per S2 says the family invented the charges to hide treason). Present both as "some historians".
- D4, confinement: in the Csejte fortress, which was not walled up. She received guests and wrote letters (S2), and Bledsaw says house arrest, "not walled in". Walling-up is LEGEND. She died 21 Aug 1614 (S5).
- D5, debt motive: S5 says a large debt owed by Matthias is a possible motive. INTERPRETATION, attributed.

## Must-nots for v3
- Never present the allegations, counts or Thurzó's findings as established fact. Attribute them every time ("witnesses said", "Thurzó wrote", "her servants confessed, under torture").
- The confessions were extracted under torture, so say so whenever they are used as evidence.
- Don't declare her innocent or guilty. The payoff is that the blood bath came later, not that she was framed.
- Keep the victims (young maids, later noble daughters) visually distinct from the four accused (older women plus one young man).
- Don't use 650 or 600 as a fact. Don't stage walling-up, the dwarf, or Darvulia as accused.
- The execution methods are DOCUMENTED, but under the owner's direction they stay non-graphic: the beheading as already plated, and no burning shown.
