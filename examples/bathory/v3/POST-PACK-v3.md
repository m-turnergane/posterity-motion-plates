In the spirit of Halloween weekend: the woman history calls the Blood Countess, and the trial she never got.

She was called the Blood Countess. No court ever heard her case.

Elizabeth Báthory came from one of Hungary's most powerful noble families. She was accused of burning young maids with hot irons and forcing others into freezing water. For months, rumours that young women were dying in her household spread, until they reached King Matthias of Hungary. He sent the Palatine, György Thurzó, the highest official in the kingdom, to investigate.

On 29 December 1610, Thurzó's armed men arrived at her manor in the village of Csejte (today Čachtice, Slovakia) while she was eating supper. In a letter the next day, Thurzó wrote that his men had found one girl dead and another dying. Báthory was taken up to the fortress above her home.

Four of her servants, three women and a young man, were arrested as her alleged accomplices. At Bytča they were questioned under torture on 2 January 1611 and confessed to dozens of killings. On 7 January a court sentenced them, and three were executed at once.

Báthory herself was never tried. King Matthias pressed for a trial more than once, and Thurzó prevented it. Thurzó had served against the Ottomans in the same war as her husband, Ferenc Nádasdy. On 3 January 1604, the day before he died, Ferenc wrote to his friend Thurzó asking him to watch over his widow and children. Many historians think Thurzó kept her confined quietly rather than risk her beheading and the loss of the family's lands. More than three hundred witnesses were questioned in the inquiries, and much of what they said was hearsay. Nothing was ever proven against her in court.

She died in confinement at Csejte on 21 August 1614.

The detail that made her infamous came much later. In 1729, about 115 years after her death, the Jesuit priest László Turóczi wrote that she had bathed in murdered girls' blood to keep her beauty. None of the witnesses against her in 1610 and 1611 had described any such thing. Her guilt was never tested in court. The legend got its monster anyway.
