They went looking for Rome. Legend says they attacked the wrong city, and history may have crowned the wrong Viking.

The fleet was real. The Annales Bertiniani, Frankish annals written at the time, say that in 859 Danish pirates sailed between Spain and Africa, entered the Rhône, raided towns and monasteries, and made their base on the island called the Camargue. In 860 they raided as far as Valence, then went to Italy and took Pisa and other cities. The annals never name the fleet's captain.

The funeral story comes later. Dudo of Saint-Quentin, a canon writing for the dukes of Normandy around 996 to 1015, tells how the raider Hastein reached a walled city by the sea and believed it was Rome. Hastein sent word that he was dying and wished to become a Christian, and the bishop and count let him be baptised. Then, on his orders, his men announced his death, asked for him to be buried in the city, and carried him in on a bier with his weapons. Mid-funeral, Dudo says, Hastein leapt down and struck the bishop. Only after the city fell, in Dudo's account, did the raiders learn it was not Rome but Luna, an old Roman town on the Italian coast. Dudo is the oldest surviving telling, and he never mentions Björn.

Decades later, around 1050 to 1070, the monk William of Jumièges retold the story. In his version the gates are barred, Hastein lies in a coffin, and the whole expedition is meant to win Rome for Bier, son of King Lothroc: Björn, son of Ragnar Lothbrok. That is how Björn Ironside entered the legend.

So the Vikings may have taken the wrong Rome, and history may have given the credit to the wrong Viking. No record from the time says who led the fleet.
