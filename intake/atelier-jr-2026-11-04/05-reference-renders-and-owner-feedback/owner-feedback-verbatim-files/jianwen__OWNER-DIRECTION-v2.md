## Proposed revised narration (owner)
They buried a body as China's emperor. His own uncle kept searching for him.

The missing emperor was Jianwen. His grandfather had founded the Ming dynasty. But Jianwen inherited something else: uncles with armies.

When he tried to take away their power, one uncle, Zhu Di, rebelled.

Three years later, Zhu Di reached Nanjing. A commander who had once fought for Jianwen helped open the city gates.

Then the palace caught fire.

Zhu Di's men carried out a body. He had it buried as his nephew.

Jianwen's seven-year-old son vanished too.

Zhu Di took the throne as Yongle, and wiped Jianwen's reign from the calendar.

But rumours said Jianwen had escaped, dressed as a monk.

Yongle sent an official named Hu Ying across China. Officially, he was seeking a Daoist holy man. Secretly, he was looking for Jianwen.

When Hu Ying's mother died, he asked to go home. Yongle refused.

More than twenty years after the fire, Hu Ying returned. Yongle was already in bed. He got up, summoned the official, and listened until deep into the night.

The later History of Ming says Yongle's suspicions finally ended. It never says what Hu Ying told him.

History remembers the victors. We remember the rest.
