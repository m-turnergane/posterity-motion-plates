# POSTERITY STANDING LAW — RETENTION, MOTION & SCORE
**Effective:** Day 11 onward (`ani_bagratid` first apply)  
**Owner lock:** 2026-09-27  
**Applies to:** INFO PACKET gate · owner plate briefs · Atelier rough · Vellum rough approval · Pulse opening diagnostics

---

## 1. First 3 seconds = separate creative deliverable
Vellum must explicitly select a **HOOK HERO** before approving a sequence plan.

The opening image should normally be the strongest, most immediately legible visual in the entire Short — scale, danger, beauty, contradiction, human action, or transformation.

**Do not** open on engraving, manuscript, generic ruin, diagram, map, establishing geography, or archival texture unless that image itself is genuinely the strongest hook available.

Mute test: a viewer seeing frame 1 **without audio** should have a reason to hesitate before swiping.

## 2. Motion from frame one
Every Short must contain **authored motion** during approximately the first 0–3 sec. Not frantic editing.

**Approved grammar:**
- slow depth/camera push
- 2.5D parallax
- animated mask/reveal
- cartographic or architectural line trace
- restrained light/shadow/environmental motion
- typographic reveal
- transformation between documented present and reconstruction where justified

**Banned:** micro-shake · random zooms · generic social-media transition packs.

VO begins immediately. Never delay the story for branding.

## 3. Recovered Chronicle title reveal
Where useful, opening may carry a **2–5 word episode-specific stake/title** (not necessarily the upload title).

Typography enters through the visual world — not a generic banner. Examples: uncovered by dust, traced by a map line, emerging from water, resolved from architectural geometry, passing through shadow.

**Target visibility:** ~0.4–2.2 sec. Readable once, then disappear.  
Posterity branding identifiable without copying another channel's banner/color treatment.

**Colleague lean (optional grammar, not mandatory clone):** “Recovered Chronicle Reveal” — striking HOOK HERO already on screen; subtle 4–7% camera push; episode-specific graphical element (traced border / shadow / smoke / waterline / dust / cartographic line / architectural outline) causes short 2–4 word title to resolve; type uncovered, not bouncing; no rectangle; no giant logo; no pause in narration.

## 4. Early visual momentum
First ~10–15 seconds require a deliberate sequence of **NEW visual information**.  
Do not hold several seconds on cosmetically different versions of the same plate.  
Maps and explanatory diagrams generally **follow** the emotional/visual hook, not precede it.

**Each sequence brief must state:**
- **RETENTION FUNCTION**
- **NEW INFORMATION**
- **MOTION OPPORTUNITY**
- **VISUAL DELTA FROM PRIOR SHOT**

**Plate gen for animation (owner briefs):** foreground/midground/background separation; no critical subject under title-safe space; extra edge room for pushes; compositions that survive a ~105–110% crop.

## 5. Music must drive, not sedate
“Restrained” ≠ sleepy. Every episode needs an **ENERGY ARC**, not merely an ambient bed:

| Beat | Energy |
|------|--------|
| HOOK | immediate audible identity/pulse |
| BUILD | rhythmic or harmonic forward motion |
| TURN/DECLINE | tension / change in texture |
| PAYOFF | controlled release / resolution |

Use rhythm, ostinato, percussion, pulse, changing instrumentation or harmonic movement where appropriate. Avoid an uninterrupted elegiac drone under an entire Short.

Music stays culturally/historically sympathetic — not costume music, trailer bombast, or regional stereotype. VO intelligibility remains primary.

**ONE EPISODE = ONE PRIMARY SCORE (owner lock 2026-10-07):** Use one musical composition/cue from opening through end card; shape ENERGY ARC with automation, EQ, stems, percussion, restrained dropouts, and sound design. Do not change songs at sequence boundaries. A second score cue requires explicit owner approval. Full law: `STANDING-LAW-one-primary-score.md`.

## 6. Hard rough-cut gate
Vellum may **not** approve a rough solely because history, images, and edit are technically correct.

Before approval ask:
1. Would frame 1 stop a Shorts-feed swipe?
2. Is something perceptibly changing by second 1?
3. Is the story already underway before second 2?
4. Does the first 10 seconds contain visual progression?
5. Does the score create forward momentum?
6. Is the opening among the strongest moments of the entire Short?

If not → return the **OPENING only** for revision before polishing the remainder.

## 7. Pulse diagnostics (post-publish)
Compare opening treatments against:
- Viewed vs swiped / engaged views
- early retention drop
- average % viewed
- rewatches / spikes

**Objective:** controlled curiosity — not faster editing for its own sake.

## 8. Motion system (Atelier standing intent)
Prefer a small **deterministic** motion primitive set reused across episodes rather than freestyle “make it dynamic” each day. Target primitives (build once, combine per episode):
- `depth_push`
- `mask_reveal`
- `line_trace`
- `type_resolve`

Remotion (if TS/React pipeline) or FFmpeg primitives are acceptable. Consistency without identical intros.
