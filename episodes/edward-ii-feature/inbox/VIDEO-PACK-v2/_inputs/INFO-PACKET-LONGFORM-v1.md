- Project: Posterity long-form feature, about 10 minutes of VO. Scriptor writes from this pack; every claim line is countersigned against CLAIM-LEDGER-v1.md.
- Prepared: Wed 4 Nov 2026 (EST, UTC-5), box research only, public sources only, no logins. No existing file was edited.
- Companion files: `CLAIM-LEDGER-v1.md` (row IDs cited below), `DECISION-PACKET-v1.md`, `MAP-PLACES-v1.md`, `REALMS-1327-v1.md`, `raw/` (captures), `../../standalones/edward-ii/` (standalone pack, S-numbers).
- Quotation rule: every string inside double quotes in this file is copied verbatim from a saved capture in `raw/` of this folder or `../../standalones/edward-ii/raw/`. OCR is quoted as saved, errors included (long s printed as f, thorn as p, footnote digits fused to words, for example "enselede28"). Our own VO lines and DO-NOT-SAY phrasings are never in double quotes. Paths are relative to this folder unless stated.
- Bands: **DOCUMENTED** (an official record says it) / **CONTEMPORARY CLAIM** (a fourteenth century chronicle, confession or letter says it; attribute it) / **INTERPRETATION** (a modern historian's judgement, or ours; attribute it) / **UNCERTAIN** (not verified, or the sources conflict) / **LEGEND** (a later story with no good source).

OPEN = ledger `OPEN`; per STANDING-LAW-longform section 4, OPEN blocks narration.
- Ledger size: CLAIM-LEDGER-v1.md has **43** rows (Part D says 43), not 39.
- Two Mortimers: always "Roger Mortimer" for the baron, "the historian Ian Mortimer" for the modern writer.
- House rules applied: no supernatural presented as fact; no gore (the poker story is named only as a later chronicle tale, never described); survival after 1327 is an attributed hypothesis; no on-screen status labels; map text is place names only; no em or en dashes in this file.

### 1c. Where the ledger and the sources disagree, or the ledger misses something (found this session)

1. **Death date vs the brief.** The brief says the announced death was 21 Sept 1327. Murimuth p. 53 has "Postea, x. kalendas Octobris" (22 Sept by the Roman count). Wikipedia (raw/wikipedia-Edward_II.noref.txt) has "On 23 September Edward III was informed that his father had died at Berkeley Castle during the night of 21 September." Treat 21 Sept as the date the government gave (UNCERTAIN, C20).
2. **Murimuth on custody and Corfe, not in the ledger.** Murimuth p. 52 (raw/admurimuthconti00robegoog_djvu.txt) says custody was ordered for Berkeley and Maltravers ("dominus Thomas de Berkeley et dominus J[ohannes] Mautravers custodiam ipsius haberent"), that he was brought to Berkeley in secret around Palm Sunday, and that for fear of rescue he was moved by night to Corfe and other secret places ("videlicet ad Corf* et aliqua alia loca secreta") before being brought back to Berkeley. So a contemporary chronicler independently puts Edward at Corfe in 1327. Recommend a new ledger row.
3. **The Berkeley verdict and the named keepers, not in C01a.** The same roll entry (Rot. Parl. ii 57) records a jury of knights finding Berkeley "in nullo eft culpabilis" of the death, says he was ill "apud Bradeleye" so that his life was despaired of, and names the keepers he had appointed, "Thomiam de Guriieyé, & Willm de Ocle, ad cuftodiam", through whom the king "extitit murdratus". The verdict is dated to the octave of St Hilary (January 1331). Recommend adding to C01a.
4. **Method of death.** The roll says only "murdre & tue" (ii 52). Murimuth's summary of Roger Mortimer's charges says the father of the king had been "fuerat suffocatus" (p. 63). That is a chronicler's paraphrase, not the roll (CONTEMPORARY CLAIM). Do not narrate a method.
5. **Deveril and Maltravers condemned for Kent's death, not in the ledger.** Rot. Parl. ii (between the p. 51 and p. 55 page markers, lines 10378 to 10393 of raw/10213330bsb_djvu.txt): "Qe Johan Mautravers fi eft cupable de la mort Efmon" (Counte de Kent), and the same judgment is given "de Boeges de Bayons, & Johan Deveroill, par la" (same cause). Kent's widow's petition (ii 55) lists "Johan Deve-" "roil" among those who gave Kent to understand his brother lived. Recommend a new row; this is DOCUMENTED and safer than the Brut for Deveril's role.
6. **Kent at Corfe in person.** Ledger C09 does not say it, but the Brut does: "til fat he come to2 pe castel of Corf*" (Brut ch. ccxxi p. 264). The decision packet's *Kent at Corfe* (packet wording) is therefore supported as a single chronicle's claim.
7. **The Brut contradicts itself on where Edward died.** Ch. ccxv says he was taken to Corfe and killed there (Brie's running head reads "Hoy} Edward II was murderd in Corfe Castle"). Ch. ccxxi says he was taken from Corfe "pe castel of Berkelee prou^ Sir* Thomas Gurnay" until he was dead. Do not use the Brut for the place of death.
8. **William de Clif.** Ian Mortimer (raw/mortimer-death-of-edward-ii.txt) says Melton's letter asked Swanland to assist "William de Clif"; Warner says Melton's messenger was "William Cliff". Kent's confession (Murimuth app. p. 254) names a "sire William de Clif" coming to Kent from la Zouche. That they are the same man is our inference only (INTERPRETATION, not verified). Do not narrate a link.
9. **Melton's year and Swanland's term.** TNA C 241 entries (raw/tna-discovery-simon-swanland-2026-10-30.txt) show recognisances taken before "Simon de Swanland, Mayor of London" on dates from 01/10/1329 to 16/10/1330 and none earlier in the capture. If he was not mayor in January 1329, the letter's year is 1330. That is our reading of a catalogue (INTERPRETATION), not a published finding.
10. **Germain's Ireland months.** The saved Germain OCR reads "per vint menses"; ledger C03b reports viiii (image check) vs viii (Stubbs). Not resolvable from the OCR; keep C03b UNCERTAIN.
11. **Haines's abstract says "the duke of Kent".** Kent was an earl. Do not copy "duke".
12. **The Fieschi letter itself names Avignon on the route ("venit Avinionem").** The brief's no Avignon applies to where the letter was written (C14b), not to the route the letter claims. Avignon may be named only as a stop in the letter's story, attributed.
13. **Cited files.** All ledger-cited raw files exist (including raw/bl-searcharchives-041-001102575.txt, raw/stubbs-chronicles-edw-i-ii-vol2-1883_djvu.txt, raw/warner-blog-search-melton.txt, raw/wikidata/). The feature verifier `raw/_verify_quotes.py` passes 207/207 across ledger, map and realms files this session.

## 8. VO-SAFE LINES (hinges)

1. Berkeley: The parliament roll records Lord Berkeley saying he never consented to the death, and never knew of it until that parliament. Historians disagree about what he meant. (C01a, C01b)
2. Death date: Edward was said to have died in late September 1327; word reached the young king at Lincoln a couple of days later. (C20)
3. Burial: He was buried at Gloucester that December. (C06)
4. Melton: A letter from the archbishop of York, which still survives, reported news that the former king was alive. (C02a)
5. Kent's informants: According to a chronicle and to his own confession, Kent was told his brother lived, and sent a sealed letter that ended up with Mortimer. (C09)
6. Kent's letter: One chronicle claims to quote how the letter began. (C05c)
7. Confession: His confession was put before the parliament at Winchester. (C05f)
8. Execution: Just over two years after the funeral, Kent was beheaded at Winchester. (C06, C28)
9. Mortimer's charges: When Roger Mortimer fell, the charges said he had the old king murdered at Berkeley, and that his people tricked Kent into believing his brother lived. (C12, C05e)
10. Fieschi: A notary of the pope, Manuel Fieschi, wrote to the young king. (C14b) His letter claims the former king escaped Berkeley, hid at Corfe, then wandered through Ireland, the Low Countries, Avignon and Germany to hermitages in Lombardy. (C03a) The letter survives only as a copy made decades later. (C14, C14b)
11. Debate: Most historians hold that Edward died at Berkeley in 1327. The historian Ian Mortimer argues the death was faked. (C16e, C16b)
12. Poker: Years later, chroniclers told a lurid story about how he died. Most historians do not believe it. (Wikipedia: "Most historians now dismiss this account of Edward's death")
