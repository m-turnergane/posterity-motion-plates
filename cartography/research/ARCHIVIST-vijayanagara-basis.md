## TOP SUMMARY (10 lines)
1. The anchor facts are firm: the Deccan sultanate alliance defeated Vijayanagara's regent Aliya Rama Raya in January 1565; Hampi (Vijayanagara) was pillaged and abandoned as capital; the court relocated to Penukonda. UNESCO, Sewell and Wikipedia all agree on that spine (DOCUMENTED, high).
2. Battle date: 23 January 1565 (Tuesday, Julian) is the best-supported; Sewell notes Firishta's weekday ("Friday") does not match his day of month, which is why 26 January circulates (QUALIFIED, medium; SEW:12912).
3. Battle SITE is the weakest point on the map: sources give Talikota, Rakkasagi-Tangadigi and Banahatti, and Sewell says the fight was "many miles to the south of the river"; Talikota town is on the Doni, not the Krishna. Show an approximate zone, never a pin on the battlefield (CONTESTED, low).
4. No contemporary map gives frontiers. Wikimedia maps checked: the "Deccan Sultanates in 16th Century" map (CC BY 4.0, redrawn from a 1949 atlas, rights uncertain) and a c.1485 CC0 map; neither is safe to copy. Show boundaries as soft shaded zones, with the Raichur doab (Krishna/Tungabhadra) as a stippled contested zone.
5. Routes: allies mustered at Bijapur, left 25 Dec 1564, reached the Krishna near Talikota, crossed by a ruse (Firishta via Sewell); Vijayanagara forces came north from the capital. Endpoints are documented; every line between them is approximate. Post-battle sultanate advance via Mudkal, Tavurugiri, Kanakagiri to Anegundi is given by Sewell's note (QUALIFIED).
6. Everything narrative comes from the victors' side (Firishta, Ahmadnagar and Bijapur court historians) or the Portuguese (Couto); Sewell (1900) compiles them; Nuniz (1520s) pre-dates the battle. No Vijayanagara-side battle account is in our captures (UNKNOWN).
7. Troop numbers are huge and inconsistent (Couto 600,000 foot + 100,000 horse; Firishta 900,000 infantry; Sewell says do not trust them). Do not put numbers on the map (CONTESTED, low).
8. Contested items that must be dotted or omitted: Muslim generals' defection (Gilani brothers), Venkatadri's fate, who sacked the city (robber tribes first, then allied troops, per Sewell; modern archaeologists limit destruction to the royal centre), sack length (five months Sewell, six months UNESCO). Rama Raya's death: show a marker, no gore.
9. Visual grammar: solid line = documented point to point; dashed = approximate; no arrow = unknown; fuzzy fill for frontiers; no unit positions, no formations.
10. Rights: build the base from Natural Earth (public domain, terms captured) and draw overlays ourselves; Sewell is public domain (Gutenberg: public domain in the USA); do not copy Britannica (not captured, 403) or the Wikimedia maps. Best other campaigns for the map style: Roanoke/Croatoan (John White's voyages 1587 and 1590) and Blackbeard's last hunt (Maynard 17 to 22 Nov 1718). Clearest for a short PoC: Blackbeard's last hunt.

**Recommended visual grammar for the PoC.**
- SOLID line: documented point-to-point where both ends are known AND the link is directly reported (for example, nothing in this pack qualifies for the army routes; use solid only for fixed features such as rivers, and for the relocation endpoints Hampi and Penukonda as pins).
- DASHED line: approximate movement, documented origin and destination but unknown path (all army arrows; royal flight; sultan advance via Mudgal; allied march from Bijapur).
- DOTTED fuzzy ellipse or hatch: uncertain place (battle site, ford, plunder extent).
- NO arrow: unknown (Vijayanagara army's road to the river, detailed movements, anyone's exact position on the field).
- Frontiers: soft-edged fills, with a stippled contested doab; legend says "approximate".
- Do not place unit icons, formations, casualty numbers, or a visible head or execution.
