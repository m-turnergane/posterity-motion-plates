# Posterity music sources shortlist (research, 2026-10-16)

Web research only. Nothing downloaded, installed, or used; nobody contacted; no Google account touched. Public pages only.
Verified = I fetched or saw the live page on 2026-10-16 and quote it. UNVERIFIED = I could not fetch the license text, so do not rely on it.
Owner correction applied: intense, driving, epic, dark and tense options are included. Each entry carries a mood tag and an intensity 1-5. The one-score law (one continuous bed, constant level, no stretch/rides/spot ducks) still applies. "Cymbal/riser" notes come from catalog descriptions only. I did not listen to audio, so they must be ear-checked.

## SUMMARY (ranked shortlist)
1. **Scott Buckley** (CC BY 4.0, verified): cinematic strings from calm/somber to epic. Best new source. Has a Smart Content ID that claims videos with no credit in the description (easy release form). Not CC BY-SA.
2. **Chris Zabriskie** (CC BY 4.0, verified): ambient, drone and eerie. Calm to somber beds, intensity 1-3. Cleanest terms, with a verbatim credit block.
3. **Alexander Nakarada / CreatorChords** (CC BY 4.0, verified): medieval, fantasy, battle and epic. Intensity 2-5. The OLD credit URL serpentsoundstudios.com was hijacked, so use creatorchords.com only. His page says use the built-in library for Shorts and TikTok.
4. **Kevin MacLeod** (CC BY 4.0, verified, stays in rotation): huge catalog. Already used for 8+ tracks.
5. **Komiku** (CC0 on FMA albums, verified): no credit needed. Folk/RPG/chiptune-ish. Check each track. Modest fit.
6. **Tim Beek** (CC BY 4.0 on "Creative Commons"-filtered tracks, verified via his Q&A and itch.io): cinematic adventure and epic. Premium tracks are NOT free.
7. **Audionautix / Jason Shaw** (CC BY 4.0, verified): broad catalog with Slow/Medium/Fast and mood filters. Fit for dark beds unverified.
8. **Karl Casey @ White Bat Audio** (custom credit license, NOT CC, verified): darksynth/retro horror, tense/driving. Modification is not explicitly stated in the license snippet, so confirm before use.
9. **Lee Rosevere** (CC BY on FMA albums, verified at album level): calm/ambient/podcast beds, intensity 1-2. Check each track.
10. **Sappheiros via Free-Stock-Music** (CC BY 3.0, verified): slow ambient, intensity 1-2. Also covers other FSM artists (Alex-Productions, Purrple Cat, Mixaund). Some FSM tracks are CC BY-NC, so check each.
11. **Eric Skiff** (CC BY 4.0, verified): 8-bit chiptune. Low fit, listed for completeness.
Excluded or not recommended: Fiechters (non-commercial), Kai Engel (stepped away from CC), Podington Bear and Ketsa (mostly NC), Bensound free tier (single-video, revocable code), YouTube Audio Library (owner-only), Uppbeat free (3 downloads + 1/month), Pixabay (no attribution needed, but a different license regime). Details below.

**Top 3 to browse first:** Scott Buckley, Chris Zabriskie, Alexander Nakarada. Browse Tim Beek and Komiku next for variety.

### 1. Scott Buckley
- Catalog: https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/ (verified live, new tracks Sept 2026)
- License: CC BY 4.0. Verified at https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/faq/ ("Yes – as long as you credit me for the use of my music") and https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/copyright-claims-release/ ("my music is released under CC-BY"). License text: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
- Commercial social use: YES, including monetized YouTube. Verified quote: "Can I use your music in a commercial project for free? Yes – as long as you credit me for the use of my music."
- Modification (trim/EQ/duck/fade): CC BY allows it. The FAQ does not forbid it. Remixes by Scott are NOT CC (see risks).
- Credit format, verbatim from the claims-release page: `[Track Title] by Scott Buckley – released under CC-BY 4.0. www.scottbuckley.com.au` Required keywords in the description: "Scott Buckley", "@ScottBuckley" or "musicbyscottb". Credit must be in the YouTube description. If you can't credit, he sells licenses (https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/licensing/: Web $40 AUD/track, incl. YouTube whitelisting).
- Content ID: HIGH awareness. He uses "Smart Content ID" and says "We Are Era Music BV, claimed on behalf of Scott Buckley". The system scans the description for a credit and claims if none is found. Release: add the credit, fill the Claim Release Form (released within 1 business day), then dispute in Content ID. A false "Sentric Music Publishing" claim can also appear. Use the same form, choose Sentric, and dispute. Search his library to confirm the track is CC (the page says "The track you chose is not from my Creative Commons library" is a common cause of claims). TikTok and IG: the system is YouTube-based. I found no TikTok or IG instructions (UNVERIFIED).
- Mood and browse (tags from his catalog descriptions, verified): Katabasis I / Anabasis I (eerie to somber, strings, intensity 2-3); Memories of Stone (somber/ancient, intensity 2); Penumbra (somber/calm, 1-2); Unraveling (calm/somber, 1-2); Eyes In The Void (tense/dark, 4-5); Song Of The Forge (epic/driving, 5, ringing anvils). Browse the Classical / Cinematic / Ambient tags.
- Starting points only: Memories Of Stone (somber, 2), Anabasis I (eerie, 2-3), Eyes In The Void (tense/dark, 4-5). All at https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/ (find by title).
- Cymbal/riser: orchestral hybrid tracks likely have risers and percussion. Strings-only works (Memories of Stone, Penumbra, Anabasis I, Unraveling) are the safest.
- Risks: remixes are not CC. No distribution via DistroKid/Spotify. Cannot resell standalone. Credit typos (e.g. misspelled name) cause claims. Track length: Unraveling is 7:24 per Free-Stock-Music listing.
- Previously used: NO.

## SOURCES CONSIDERED AND NOT RECOMMENDED / OWNER-ONLY

- **Pixabay** (verified https://pixabay.com/service/terms/ section 5 and https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/): Pixabay Content License. "We grant you an irrevocable, worldwide, perpetual… right to download, use, copy, modify or adapt the Content for commercial or non-commercial purposes"; "You do not need to credit Pixabay or the contributor". Prohibited: selling the content on a Standalone basis; use "in a misleading or deceptive way". Optional credit: `by [Contributor] via Pixabay`. Not CC. No attribution (the brief wanted attribution-only; this is more permissive but a different regime). Content ID risk: not verified, contributors upload to distributors at times, so search the track on YouTube. Content published before 9 Jan 2019 is CC0. License can be changed by Pixabay at any time (section 1).
- **Uppbeat free tier** (verified https://uppbeat.io/free , https://uppbeat.io/uppbeat-basic-license , https://uppbeat.io/pricing): 3 downloads then +1 per month, ~30% of catalog, individual creators on their own channels. Free users "must ensure that the appropriate Credit is given whenever you use the Music. This is a material term." A new credit is generated per download, needed for each video, and covers YouTube and TikTok. No safelisting on free. Verdict: too limited for a 40-60 Shorts/month pipeline, and the account is a login. Premium (Essentials $6.99/mo) removes credit via safelist for 1 YouTube channel, which is paid and out of scope.
- **Bensound free** (verified https://www.bensound.com/terms-and-conditions , revised 2026-06-22, section 4.2): Free License is "revocable", requires "the attribution text and its unique license code provided when downloading", "valid for a single video only", and covers "online video… published AND accessible free of charge". Not recommended: per-video code, revocable, and the wording about being free of charge is ambiguous for monetized Shorts. (https://www.bensound.com/licensing now 404s.)
- **YouTube Audio Library** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882, verified): "The Audio Library is found exclusively in YouTube Studio", so it needs a signed-in Google account. **OWNER-ONLY. Agents must not access it.** Terms: tracks have either a standard Audio Library license (attribution not required) or CC (credit in the description via the "Copy" button). "Copyright-safe music… won't be claimed by a rights holder through the Content ID system." That holds only on YouTube, not TikTok/IG. Direct fetch of youtube.com/audiolibrary returned 403.
- **Fiechters (Derek & Brandon Fiechter)**: UNVERIFIED on their own page (not fetched). Search results state non-commercial use with credit "Derek Fiechter & Brandon Fiechter", contact dbfiechter@gmail.com. Non-commercial fails the brief. Only usable with an explicit written commercial license from them. Intense/epic fantasy fit is strong, so email them later via the owner if wanted.
- **Kai Engel** (verified https://www.kai-engel.com/): "I'm gradually stepping away from Creative Commons and will no longer offer my music or support for new projects under this license." Releases before 2025-07-01 may still be CC, but FMA albums are mixed (Rain Catcher: Attribution-NonCommercial; Traffic: CC BY). Excluded because of NC mix and no support.
- **Podington Bear / Ketsa**: UNVERIFIED current licenses. FMA's own examples show Podington Bear as "CC BY-NC" and Ketsa "Single Steps" as "CC BY-NC-ND" (https://freemusicarchive.org/faq , https://freemusicarchive.org/license_guide). Excluded: NC/ND. Check per track only if desired.
- **FMA in general** (verified https://freemusicarchive.org/faq): per-track licenses. Use the License filter. "Share-Alike" tracks "can't [be used] without additional permissions" unless your work is also under the same license. FMA cannot resolve Content ID claims and tells you to contact the artist.
- **Chosic** (https://chosic.com/free-music/all/, listing fetched): aggregator with tags (medieval, historical, dark, ambient, cinematic, for tiktok). Each track says "free to use if you credit the artist". Per-track credit text only. License terms page not fetched, so UNVERIFIED as a source. Use only to discover artists, then verify at the artist's page.
- **Musopen** (https://musopen.org/about/): non-profit. "We provide recordings, sheet music, and textbooks to the public for free, without copyright restrictions." ## GENERAL: Content ID with attribution-licensed music
- How claims happen: the music owner (or a distributor) registers audio fingerprints with YouTube Content ID. Any upload that matches gets a claim, whether or not it follows the license. Some artists, like Scott Buckley and Nakarada, use a Smart Content ID that scans the description for a credit and claims only when it is missing. Others (Zabriskie) say claims are NOT from them, meaning third parties made them. FMA warns that "some artists… have started using YouTube's Content ID" and that this "does not conflict with the terms of a Creative Commons license".
- How to dispute: (1) Confirm the credit is in the description exactly as the artist templates it. (2) Use the artist's own release form where one exists (Buckley's Claim Release Form; Nakarada's form; White Bat's claims form). (3) Dispute in YouTube Studio, quoting the license URL and the artist's page. (4) For third-party/false claims, email the artist (Zabriskie, Casey and Buckley all ask for this) with the claim details. Per Buckley, disputing also keeps revenue in escrow until release.
- Keep a license log per track: date downloaded, source URL (artist page, not just an aggregator), license name and URL, a full-page screenshot showing the license and track title, the downloaded file md5, ISRC if present, and the exact credit text used. Posterity's bed_LICENSE files already do most of this. Kevin MacLeod's FAQ explicitly recommends printing the CC legalcode and the track page showing the URL and CC logo. Also capture a screenshot of the artist's license/credit instructions page, since terms change (Nakarada moved domains; Kai Engel withdrew from CC; Bensound revised its terms on 2026-06-22 and the old /licensing URL now 404s).
- Search every candidate on YouTube first for existing claims and for other channels reusing it, as a rough indicator of claim risk.

## GENERAL: TikTok and Instagram specifics
- Credit in the caption/description. Neither platform has an external credit field on the video itself. For CC BY, a reasonable-in-context credit (CC BY 4.0 section 3(a)(2)) in the caption satisfies the license, but captions can be truncated. Put the credit early or in a pinned comment, plus on the Posterity YouTube description, as extra.
- Library vs external upload differs: Nakarada says "For YouTube Shorts and TikTok please use their built in library (my music is available there)", and that his Smart Content ID affects "YouTube, Facebook and Instagram". Most other artists give no TikTok/IG instructions (UNVERIFIED). Uppbeat free users need the credit in the description on TikTok too.
- The credit is a license obligation, so use the same credit text on all three platforms, adding the "Changes:" line.

## HOW TO VET A TRACK (checklist)
1. License page check: open the artist's own page (or FMA track page), confirm CC BY / CC0 / custom terms, copy the exact credit text, screenshot it. Reject NC, ND, SA (SA is permitted by the license text only if your adaptation is also SA, which is a problem for the video), and "all rights reserved without a license page".
2. Check the track-level license, not just the album (FMA, FSM, Chosic, Tim Beek Premium vs CC).
3. Content ID check: search the track title plus artist on YouTube. Look for other channels with claims, "Content ID" notes, or a distributor name in the credits.
4. Calmness screen by ear (human): no cymbals, risers or big hits (unless the story needs it per the owner's correction), constant level, no mid-track drop. Note intensity 1-5 and mood tag.
5. Length: at least package + 10 s native, with no stretch (per the lock).
6. Record in bed_LICENSE_*.txt with the artist's credit format plus "Changes: trimmed to X s, EQ, level, ducking and fade-out."
